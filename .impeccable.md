## Design Context

### Users
LUC undergrad students navigating complex overlapping degree requirements. They're stressed about graduation timelines, juggling multiple majors/minors, and trying to make sense of a constraint satisfaction problem they didn't sign up for. They use this on their phones between classes and on laptops during advising sessions. The job: figure out the most efficient path to graduation without missing requirements or wasting course slots.

### Brand Personality
**Friendly, playful, casual.** "Your smart friend made this" — not a startup, not a university admin tool. Irreverent where appropriate, but never at the expense of clarity. The kind of tool that makes you feel like someone who actually understands the system built it for you personally.

### Emotional Goals
**Delight & surprise.** Students should think "this is way cooler than I expected for a university tool." Moments of joy in the details — micro-interactions, clever copy, satisfying state transitions. Reduce anxiety by making complex information feel approachable and even fun.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **References:** Are.na / Cargo energy — editorial, warm, anti-corporate
- **Anti-references:** Generic SaaS dashboards, cool-gray tech tools, anything that looks like it came from a startup template
- **Typography:** Serif display (Source Serif 4) paired with monospace data (DM Mono) — the tension between scholarly and technical is the signature
- **Color philosophy:** Warm parchment base (`#fffbf0`), not cool grays. Color used sparingly and purposefully for program identity (maroon PLSC, green GLST, etc.) and status. No gradients, no decorative color.
- **Density:** Dense but not cluttered — closer to a well-designed academic planner than a tech dashboard. Information-rich without overwhelm.
- **Theme:** Light mode only. The warm background IS the brand.

### Design Principles

1. **Warm over cold.** Every surface should feel inviting — parchment tones, serif warmth, rounded but not bubbly. Never sterile, never corporate.

2. **Clarity through typography, not chrome.** Let the Source Serif / DM Mono pairing do the heavy lifting. Hierarchy comes from weight, size, and font choice — not borders, backgrounds, or decorative elements.

3. **Color means something.** Every color in the palette is earned — program identity, course status, error states. No decorative color. When color appears, it communicates.

4. **Dense but breathable.** Students need to see a lot of information at once. Pack it in, but give it rhythm — consistent spacing, clear grouping, white space where it matters.

5. **Delightful details.** The personality lives in small moments: a satisfying animation, a clever label, an unexpected touch that makes a student smile. These aren't decoration — they're what makes the tool feel like it was made by a person, not a committee.
